> **Summary.** AI roleplay where you play your own character splits the story in two: the player writes one character's words, thoughts and actions, and the AI writes the world and every other character. The split holds when the AI is told on every reply which character belongs to the player and where to stop, which is what MC Mode does on Lunafic.

> Written by the Lunafic team. [Lunafic](https://lunafic.ai) is our AI roleplay app, and this guide describes it alongside the general method. Facts last checked October 25, 2026.

## Overview

In AI roleplay, the player character is the one character a person controls. Everyone else in the story, from the rival at the next desk to the guard at the gate, is written by the AI.

The most common failure is the reverse. The AI answers with the other character's line, then keeps going and writes the player character's reaction, a decision, or a full line of dialogue. Players often call this "speaking for me". It happens because a language model continues text. Most stories it learned from show both sides of a conversation, so finishing both sides is the easy path unless something says otherwise.

A request typed into the chat, such as "do not speak for me", is one line in a growing transcript, and it fades as the story gets longer. A lasting fix belongs in the instructions the AI receives with every reply.

## What belongs to whom

**The player writes:**

- what the player character says and does
- what the player character thinks and feels
- every decision the player character makes

**The AI writes:**

- every other character's lines and actions
- the setting, and events in the world
- what the player character can see and hear

The AI may describe a door slamming or the look on another face. It may not say how the player character feels about it, or what they do next.

## How Lunafic does it: MC Mode

MC stands for main character. [MC Mode](https://lunafic.ai/features/mc-mode/) is the default for every new story on Lunafic, and the [documentation](https://lunafic.ai/docs/two-ways-to-direct) describes four parts:

1. **The AI never writes the player's side.** Not their dialogue, thoughts or actions. The player's lines appear as that character, with name and portrait.
2. **The AI plays everyone else.** Each character answers in their own speech style and language.
3. **The AI hands the turn back.** It writes what the others do and what the player character can perceive, then stops where that character has to act, speak or decide.
4. **The AI sees the scene from inside.** If the player character only knows someone as "the man in the mask", that is all the AI knows about him too.

MC Mode is enforced through the instructions the AI receives on every reply. It is not a filter on the output. It usually holds and can still slip now and then, which is why every line stays editable.

There are several ways to choose who the player character is:

- **A role in a Playable World.** The player picks one of the roles the author made, then chooses a name and writes how the character speaks.
- **Play as yourself.** An option at the end of the role list, with no hero. If the author made no roles, the screen offers Write my own hero.
- **Any character in your own Private world.** Under Playing as in the right panel, Who you play lists the characters in the world's Wiki. Edit your MC changes the character's name, background, traits and portrait.

## MC Mode and Author Mode

Lunafic gives the player a second seat, Author Mode. It is only available in a Private world the player owns.

| Question | MC Mode | Author Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Where the player sits | Inside the scene, as one character | Above the scene, as the author |
| What the player types | A line or action for their character | A direction, like "Three days pass" |
| Who the AI writes | Everyone except the player character | Every character, the player's included |
| Does the typed turn appear in the story? | Yes, as the character's line | No, only the writing it produces |
| What the player knows | What their character knows | Everything, secrets included |
| Where it is available | Playable Worlds and Private worlds | Private worlds only |

Both modes share the same world, chapters and memory. A common pattern is to play in MC Mode and switch to Author Mode briefly for a time skip, using the option at the bottom of the Who you play picker.

## Example: The Flat Note

[My Liege Is Dead, Welcome to Royal Vael Akademia](https://lunafic.ai/scenarios/royal-vael-akademia) is an original fantasy world on Lunafic, set at a school where magic flows down a cord from the person you swore to. The player is Casimir Rook, a scholarship pledge from a marsh village. His old black cord belongs to a house that died out a hundred and four years ago. His liege is dead, and the magic comes anyway.

One of the world's starting choices is called The Flat Note: "In the library Casimir tells Genevieve Lathom 'It's nothing', the Bell in her ear goes flat, and she closes her ledger to find out what he lied about." Like every starting choice, it lands in the reply line as the player's first move, editable before sending.

Genevieve's open Personality panel explains the setup. She "hears lies as flat notes", and since Casimir said "it's nothing" about the cord, "she has been unable to leave it alone since". The same panel calls her "blunt, short-spoken, impatient with pleasantries".

In MC Mode the scene divides cleanly. The AI plays Genevieve: the closed ledger, the direct question, the pressure. It can describe what Casimir sees, such as her stare across the table. It does not decide whether Casimir confesses, lies again, or walks out of the library. That choice waits in the reply line.

## Step by step

1. Open a world on [lunafic.ai](https://lunafic.ai) and press **Play**. The world introduces itself; press **Begin**.
2. Pick a role card and press **Continue**, or choose to play as yourself.
3. Fill the hero sheet: name, traits, where you come from, and a portrait if you like. **Let the AI interview me** fills it from a short chat. Press **Enter the story**.
4. Under **Your first move**, tap a starting choice or write your own move in the reply line.
5. Write your character's turn. Words in asterisks are action, and words in quotes are speech. A gesture or a half-truth gives the scene something to answer.
6. Write only what your character notices. If you narrate something your character cannot know, the AI may use it too.

More detail is in [Choose who you are before the story opens](https://lunafic.ai/docs/entering-a-playable-world) and [Write a turn the AI can build on](https://lunafic.ai/docs/roleplay-writing).

## Constraints and trade-offs

- **Instructions, not a filter.** A reply can occasionally put a word in the player character's mouth. Retry writes the reply again, Remove deletes it, and any line can be fixed by hand. Typing /ooc and a note right after a reply makes the AI write that reply again with the note, which no character hears.
- **No autopilot.** The AI will not move the player character forward. A turn that says "I nod" gets an answer to a nod.
- **Author Mode is for Private worlds only.** In a Playable World the player always sits inside the scene, in the chosen role.
- **Free play has no endings.** Playing as yourself or with your own hero works in a Playable World, but its endings are written for the author's roles.
- **A young library.** Lunafic has far fewer worlds than the large chat apps, and most lean toward anime and fantasy.

## FAQ

### Can the AI ever write my character on Lunafic?

Not in MC Mode, which is the default. In Author Mode the AI writes every character, yours included, but that mode exists only in your own Private world and you have to choose it.

### Can I play a character I made myself?

Yes. In your own Private world, open Edit your MC and fill in your character, or step into any character already in your Wiki. In a Playable World you take one of the author's roles with your own name and voice, or play as yourself.

### Can other characters read my character's mind?

No. In MC Mode the AI sees the scene from inside, so other characters only know what has been said or shown in front of them. A secret stays secret until your character reveals it.
