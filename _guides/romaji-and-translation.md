## Overview

Romaji is Japanese written in the Latin alphabet. In an AI roleplay with dual-language dialogue, each spoken line can appear in romaji, with the translation in brackets under it. The romaji carries what the translation tends to lose: the character's word for "I", the name tag they put on someone, and the ending on the sentence.

The tables below use the Hepburn-style spellings that most romaji readers see. The romaji in a story is written by the AI, so spellings can vary from line to line. The meanings stay the same.

How the setting itself works is covered in the companion guide, [Japanese dialogue in AI roleplay](https://lunafic-ai.github.io/roleplay/guides/japanese-dialogue-ai-roleplay/).

## One line, piece by piece

This line is from Euler Florence's Speech Examples in [Vengeance and Love Languages](https://lunafic.ai/scenarios/vengeance-and-love-language), an original world by the Lunafic team, shown the way Romanized plus Translation lays it out:

> "Watakushi no michi wa watakushi ga kimeru."
>
> [I decide my own path.]

| Piece | Type | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| watakushi | first-person pronoun | I (very formal) |
| no | particle | 's, of |
| michi | noun | path, way |
| wa | particle | marks the topic |
| ga | particle | marks who does the action |
| kimeru | verb, plain form | decide |

The formal word for "I" sits next to a plain verb with no polite ending. The line sounds proud and final rather than polite.

## Spelling conventions

| Rule | How it is written | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Five vowels | a, i, u, e, o. Each always has the same sound, and there is no silent e. | sake is sa-ke |
| Long vowels | Doubled letters (aa, ii, uu), or ei and ou. Textbooks may use a line on top: ā, ū, ō. Length changes the word. | obasan (aunt), obaasan (grandmother); yuki (snow), yuuki (courage) |
| Double consonants | A doubled letter marks a short stop before it. Before ch it is written tch. | kite (come), kitte (stamp); matcha |
| Syllabic n | n alone is a full beat. An apostrophe before a vowel or y keeps it separate. | kin'en (no smoking), kinen (anniversary) |
| n before b, m, p | Some spellings change it to m. Both mean the same. | shinbun or shimbun (newspaper) |
| Quiet u and i | Next to some consonants, such as s, t and k, u and i are barely said. | desu sounds like "dess" |

## Particles

Particles are short words after a noun that show its job in the sentence. Three are spelled by sound, not by the kana used to write them.

| Particle | Kana | Also seen as | Job | Generic example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| wa | は | ha | topic, "as for" | Kore wa hon desu. (This is a book.) |
| o | を | wo | direct object | Mizu o nomu. (I drink water.) |
| e | へ | he | direction, "toward" | Umi e iku. (I go to the sea.) |
| ga | が | | subject, often new information | Dare ga kita? (Who came?) |
| no | の | | owner or link, like 's | Haru no kaban (Haru's bag) |
| ni | に | | place, time or target | Kyoushitsu ni iru. (I'm in the classroom.) |
| de | で | | where an action happens, or by what means | Toshokan de yomu. (I read at the library.) |
| mo | も | | also, too | Watashi mo. (Me too.) |

The generic examples are simple teaching sentences, not lines from a world.

## Honorifics

Honorifics attach to a name. When one changes during a story, the relationship has changed too.

| Suffix | Used for | Effect in fiction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| -san | Classmates, coworkers, strangers, usually after the family name | Neutral politeness. Keeps a polite distance. |
| -kun | Boys and young men, from friends, teachers or seniors | Familiar, slightly from above |
| -chan | Small children, close friends, girls, pets | Affection. On someone who did not invite it, teasing. |
| -sama | Customers, nobles, gods, a master | High respect. Used on oneself, as ore-sama, it shows arrogance. |
| -senpai | Someone ahead of the speaker at school, in a club or at work | Respect with closeness. Often used alone as a name. |
| -sensei | Teachers, doctors, writers, manga artists | Respect for skill. Often used alone as a name. |
| no suffix | Family, close friends, partners | Closeness. From a stranger, it is blunt or rude. |

One look-alike is not an honorific. **-ke** means "the house of" or "the family of". In Vengeance and Love Languages it appears as Florence-ke, the Florence family.

## Words for "I"

| Pronoun | Register | Typical speaker in anime |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| watashi | Standard and polite | Anyone in formal speech. Many women and girls in all speech. |
| watakushi | Very formal | Formal business speech. In anime, the ojou-sama, nobles and butlers. |
| boku | Soft and casual | Boys and gentle young men. Sometimes a tomboyish girl. |
| ore | Rough and casual | Confident young men, delinquents, shouting heroes. Rude in formal settings. |
| atashi | Casual | Girls and women in relaxed speech |
| washi | Old-fashioned | Old men and elders in fiction |

## Words for "you"

| Pronoun | Feel |
| :--- | :--- |
| anata | Polite, but often avoided in favor of the person's name |
| kimi | From a senior, a teacher or a calm male speaker |
| anta | Casual, can sound rude |
| omae | Rough. Close friends, or an insult. |
| kisama | Hostile. In fiction, an enemy's word. |

## Common sentence endings

| Ending | What it does | Feel |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| desu, masu | Polite form of a sentence | Calm, careful, or keeping distance |
| da | Plain form of desu | Casual and direct |
| yo | Tells the listener something new | "I'm telling you" |
| ne | Asks for agreement | "Right?", soft |
| yo ne | Checks a shared fact | "That's right, isn't it?" |
| ka | Marks a question | Neutral with desu ka, blunt in plain speech |
| no | Soft question or explanation | Gentle. Rising "no?" is a question. |
| wa | Soft ending, in fiction often stacked as desu wa | Refined and feminine. Rarer in real speech than in anime. |
| zo, ze | Strong emphasis | Rough and usually male |
| kashira | "I wonder" | Feminine and refined |
| deshou, darou | Guess, or pushes for agreement | "Probably", "surely" |
| janai | Negative, or a rising "isn't it?" | Casual |
| -nasai | Command on a verb stem | From a parent, teacher or superior. Suwarinasai. (Sit down.) |

## Turning on the romaji line in Lunafic

1. Open the right panel, then **This world**, **Narrative settings**, **Language**.
2. Set **Character Language** to Japanese and **Narrative Language** to English.
3. Set **Dialogue Format** to **Romanized plus Translation**.

In a Playable World the author sets these when building it. Vengeance and Love Languages already opens in this format. Japanese lines in dual language have a speaker button at the end that reads the line aloud with the device's own voice, which uses no AI usage.

## Constraints

- **Spelling varies.** The same word may show up as o or wo, ou or ō or oo. Read them as one word.
- **The AI can be wrong.** A line can be stiff or wrong, and the translation can be loose. Retry the reply, or edit the line by hand, which uses no AI usage.
- **Fiction speech is not everyday speech.** Many pronouns and endings here are stage voices for a character type. Real speakers use them less, or differently.
- **The tables are simplified.** Each row gives the most common use, not every use.

## FAQ

### Why is は written "wa" in romaji?

As the topic particle it is said "wa", and Hepburn spells the sound. The same kana is "ha" inside other words.

### Is "wo" a mistake?

No. It is another way to spell the object particle を. Hepburn writes it "o".

### Do I need to know these tables to play?

No. The English translation sits under every line. The tables help when the romaji shows something the translation does not.

### Can I hide the English and keep only the Japanese?

Yes. Native Only shows the line in Japanese script with no translation. There is no documented option that shows romaji without the translation.
